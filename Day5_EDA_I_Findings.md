# Findings

### 1. Churn Distribution

The overall churn rate is **26.54%**, meaning that approximately one in four customers has left the company. This indicates that customer churn is a significant business issue that requires further investigation.

### 2. Gender vs Churn

Churn rates are very similar between female and male customers (**26.92% vs. 26.16%**). Therefore, gender does not appear to be a strong differentiating factor for customer churn in this dataset.

### 3. SeniorCitizen vs Churn

Senior citizens have a substantially higher churn rate (**41.68%**) compared with non-senior customers (**23.61%**). This suggests that senior customers may represent a higher-risk segment for churn.

### 4. Partner vs Churn

Customers without a partner have a higher churn rate (**32.96%**) compared with customers with a partner (**19.66%**). This indicates that having a partner is associated with lower customer churn.

### 5. Dependents vs Churn

Customers without dependents have a churn rate of **31.28%**, more than twice the rate of customers with dependents (**15.45%**). This suggests that customers with dependents are considerably less likely to churn.

### 6. Tenure vs Churn

Customers who churned had an average tenure of **17.98 months**, compared with **37.57 months** for customers who stayed. This indicates that newer customers are substantially more likely to churn than long-term customers.

### 7. Contract vs Churn

Contract type shows one of the strongest differences in churn. Customers on month-to-month contracts have a churn rate of **42.71%**, compared with **11.27%** for one-year contracts and only **2.83%** for two-year contracts. This suggests that customers with longer-term contracts are significantly less likely to churn.

### Overall Insight

The analysis indicates that churn is particularly associated with **short customer tenure, month-to-month contracts, senior-citizen status, lack of a partner, and lack of dependents**. In contrast, gender shows very little difference in churn behavior. These findings suggest that customer retention efforts should prioritize **new customers and customers on month-to-month contracts**, while further analysis should investigate the reasons behind the higher churn among these segments.
